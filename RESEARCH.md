# Link Preview / Unfurl Research for Hotspot (meetup.sals.site)

Research date: 2026-09-06. Every claim is tagged **[verified]** (read in a primary or strong source this session), **[likely]** (consistent third-party documentation, not confirmed by a primary source), or **[unverified]** (inference or no citable source). Primary sources actually read: Apple TN3156 (full text extracted via the developer.apple.com docs JSON endpoint), Meta's WhatsApp Link Previews developer doc, Google's user-triggered fetchers doc, Cloudflare's default cache behavior doc, Mysk's link preview security research, plus the secondary sources listed at the end.

## 1. iMessage: who fetches, when, and refetch behavior

**The sender's device fetches the preview.** This is the single most important fact for a "live" og.png. Mysk's research (the canonical study of messenger preview behavior) confirms: in iMessage the sender's device fetches the URL, builds the preview, and transmits the preview itself (metadata plus image) as an attachment with the message. **[verified]** Corroborated by multiple independent writeups showing iMessage preview requests arriving from residential/mobile ISP IPs, not Apple datacenter IPs. **[verified]**

**When:** at compose time. The preview renders in the sender's compose field as soon as the URL is typed or pasted, before the message is sent, and the captured snapshot is what ships. Mysk describes the fetch as happening when the message is sent; the compose-bar render is universally observable behavior. Sender-side fetch: **[verified]**. Exact compose-vs-send timing: **[likely]**.

**The receiver does not fetch** in a normal blue-bubble conversation. The receiver renders the attached snapshot. It is never refetched, and the bubble never updates, even if your OG tags or image change later. **[verified]** (Mysk; also matches Apple forum reports of "stale" previews persisting in old threads). An older 2016-era writeup (rsmck.co.uk) documented receiver-side fetching on iOS 10; that behavior is superseded and should be ignored for 2025-2026 design. **[verified that the old claim exists; the modern model is sender-side]**

**Each Apple device of the fetching user may issue its own request** (iPhone and Mac both fetch), so expect duplicate hits per share. **[likely]**

**User agent** of the iMessage preview fetch:
`Mozilla/5.0 (Macintosh; Intel Mac OS X 10_11_1) AppleWebKit/601.2.4 (KHTML, like Gecko) Version/9.0.1 Safari/601.2.4 facebookexternalhit/1.1 Facebot Twitterbot/1.0`
The distinguishing signature is that it contains BOTH `facebookexternalhit` and `Twitterbot`, and comes from an end-user IP rather than Meta's AS32934. **[verified]** (deviceandbrowserinfo.com, corroborated by Medium writeups and ppc.land).

**Caching:** anecdotal but consistent reports of aggressive preview caching on Apple devices; third-party guides recommend cache-buster query params when testing. There is no documented TTL. Assume a given device may reuse a cached preview when the same exact URL is re-shared soon after. **[likely]** One documented "stale preview" case on Apple's forums turned out to be the site serving a "browser not supported" page to Apple's UA, not caching at all. **[verified]**

### Implication for the live og.png

The recipient sees the event state as of the moment the SENDER composed the message, frozen forever in that bubble. Everyone in a group thread sees that same snapshot. A re-share later (by anyone) produces a fresh snapshot. So:

- The OG image CAN reflect live state, but only "live as of each share". Design the image so the moment-of-share snapshot is inherently useful: event title, candidate dates, mini heatmap, "3 of 6 responded".
- The most common snapshot is the very first one: the creator sharing seconds after creating the event, with zero responses. Design a beautiful zero-state OG image (event name, dates, "be the first to pick times"), not a sad empty heatmap.
- Never rely on the embed updating. The live experience happens after the tap, in the web app.

## 2. og:image requirements for iMessage

From Apple's official technote TN3156 ("Create rich previews for Messages", republished 2024-04-30, successor to TN2444), all **[verified]**, exact wording extracted:

- "Images should be at least 900 pixels in width."
- "Images less than 150 pixels in width may be ignored or presented as icons."
- "Icons should be square, at least 108 pixels per side."
- "The main resource located at the previewed link is limited to 1 MB." (that is your HTML page)
- "The total size of associated resources (icons, images, and videos, etc) is limited to 10 MB."
- "Link previews do not follow `meta` redirects, nor run JavaScript; metadata must be available directly on the linked page. Server-side redirects are followed, however, and are a good alternative."
- "Avoid text in preview images. Images may be displayed at varying sizes depending on context and device, potentially making text unreadable." (Apple's guidance; you will violate it deliberately for the heatmap/count, keep text large)
- Serve the same metadata to all user agents; no UA tricks.
- Direct-file `og:video` / `twitter:player:stream` (e.g. an MP4 URL) downloads and autoplays inline; HLS requires a tap; embed-HTML videos do not play.

Third-party specifics (opengraphplus.com, ogpreview.app), all **[likely]**:

- 1200x630 (1.91:1) renders correctly; 1200x1200 square "survives crops" across iOS versions; iOS 17 applies aggressive square-ish cropping in some contexts; an image at least 2400x1256 triggers the full-width bubble layout introduced in iOS 16.
- JPEG and PNG are reliable. GIF shows only the first frame. WebP and SVG are inconsistent, avoid them.
- Keep the image well under 1 MB in practice, since a phone on cellular is doing the download at compose time.
- HTTPS is required; plain-HTTP image URLs will not load (consistent with iOS App Transport Security). **[likely, multiple independent sources, no official statement found]**

**Response speed:** no published timeout exists for iMessage. **[unverified]** Practical guidance: the fetch races the sender hitting send on a phone network; if the preview is not ready, the message goes out as a plain link, permanently. Target under ~500 ms TTFB for both the HTML and og.png (pre-render or cache the PNG server-side; satori+resvg at request time for a cold event should still be well under 1 s on your box). **[unverified, engineering inference]**

**Redirects:** server-side 301/302 are followed **[verified, TN3156]**. Keep the chain short; ideally the short link returns 200 with meta tags directly rather than redirecting.

## 3. Recommended meta tag set for the best iMessage card

Grounded in TN3156 **[verified]** except where noted:

```html
<title>Pizza night? · Hotspot</title>
<meta property="og:title" content="Pizza night?" />          <!-- short, no branding in it -->
<meta property="og:site_name" content="Hotspot" />           <!-- branding goes here -->
<meta property="og:description" content="Pick the times you can make. 3 of 6 have answered." />
<meta property="og:image" content="https://meetup.sals.site/e/abc123/og.png" />
<meta property="og:image:width" content="1200" />            <!-- harmless, helps other platforms -->
<meta property="og:image:height" content="630" />
<meta property="og:url" content="https://meetup.sals.site/e/abc123" />
<meta name="twitter:card" content="summary_large_image" />   <!-- for Discord/Slack/X, see below -->
<link rel="apple-touch-icon" sizes="180x180" href="/apple-touch-icon.png" />
<link rel="icon" href="/favicon.png" />
```

- `og:title`: Apple explicitly says keep it short, page-specific, and do NOT put the site name in it; use `og:site_name` for that. **[verified]**
- `apple-touch-icon`: iMessage uses an apple-touch-icon, favicon, or `<link rel>` icon as the small icon in the card, and falls back to the icon as the whole preview if og:image is missing or too small (<150px wide). Ship a 180x180 apple-touch-icon. **[verified]**
- `twitter:card`: NOT needed for iMessage on a normal page. TN3156 only requires `twitter:card` (`summary`/`summary_large_image`) plus an `application/activity+json` link for social-network-post style previews (Mastodon-like). Include `summary_large_image` anyway because Discord, Slack, and X use it. **[verified]**
- `og:description`: read by iMessage but the bubble mostly shows image + title + domain; description matters more on WhatsApp/Slack/Discord. **[likely]**
- og:image must be an absolute HTTPS URL. **[likely]**

## 5. Pitfalls

1. **SPA client-side meta tags do not work.** Apple states it flatly: link previews "do not follow meta redirects, nor run JavaScript". **[verified]** No mainstream messenger's fetcher runs JS (the only JS-executing preview fetchers Mysk found were Instagram and LinkedIn servers). **[verified]** Design consequence: your Hono server must inject per-event OG tags into the HTML it serves at `/e/:id` before the SPA bundle loads. Same HTML for bots and humans (Apple asks for identical metadata across UAs, and UA-sniffing is exactly what broke previews in the Apple forums case). **[verified]**
2. **Content-Type strictness.** og:image must return a real `image/*` content type. A known failure mode: CDN or tunnel serves an HTML error page with 200 status for the image URL, and the preview dies silently. resvg output served as `image/png` is fine. **[likely]**
3. **Cloudflare specifics, both bite you:**
   - Bot Fight Mode / Super Bot Fight Mode is repeatedly reported to block preview crawlers (facebookexternalhit, Discordbot, etc). The iMessage UA contains `facebookexternalhit` and `Twitterbot` while coming from a residential IP, which is exactly the "spoofed bot UA" pattern SBFM punishes. If you enable any bot-fighting on meetup.sals.site, add allow rules for the event and og.png routes. **[likely, multiple Cloudflare community threads]**
   - Cloudflare caches `.png` by file extension by default, and with no Cache-Control from origin the default edge TTL for a 200 is 120 minutes; it does respect origin Cache-Control. **[verified, Cloudflare docs]** Serve og.png with explicit `Cache-Control: public, max-age=60` (or `no-store` if you want every share pixel-fresh), otherwise a re-share within 2 hours shows a stale heatmap.
4. **Head placement and size.** Keep the OG block within the first few KB of `<head>`: Slack reads ~32 KB **[likely]**, WhatsApp requires the head within the first 300 KB **[verified]**, and Apple caps the whole HTML resource at 1 MB **[verified]**. Do not inline your Vite bundle or large CSS above the meta tags.
5. **Stale-forever bubbles.** Because iMessage and WhatsApp ship the preview inside the message, fixing OG tags never fixes already-sent messages. Get the OG route right before sharing real links, and use a cache-buster query param while testing on your own devices since the sender device may cache per-URL. **[likely]**
6. **One preview per message, snapshot semantics.** Do not encode anything time-sensitive-critical (like "closes in 2 hours") in the image; it will be wrong in old bubbles. Relative phrasing that ages gracefully ("5 of 6 can make Fri 7-9pm") is safer than countdowns. **[unverified, design inference]**

## 6. SMS fallback vs iMessage

When the recipient is a green bubble (Android via SMS, or iMessage unavailable), behavior changes completely:

- The sender's iPhone does not attach a preview to SMS. On the receiving iPhone side of an SMS, iOS shows a rich preview only under narrow conditions: the sender's number is a saved contact, and the URL is the last thing in the message with no trailing text or emoji; even then it often renders as "Tap to Load Preview", and the RECEIVER's device fetches when tapped. These messages are still SMS, not MMS. **[likely, Postscript and Listrak operational docs for A2P SMS; consistent across sources]**
- Unknown-sender filtering can suppress previews entirely. **[likely]**
- An Android recipient in Google Messages gets a preview generated by Google's `GoogleMessages` server-side fetcher reading your OG tags. **[verified]**
- Design consequences: the share flow's suggested text should put the link at the very end ("Pizza night? Pick your times: https://meetup.sals.site/e/abc123"), and ideally the link is its own message. The link must look trustworthy as plain text since many SMS recipients will see no card at all: keep the slug short, readable, and lowercase. When the receiver taps to load on SMS, they fetch live state, so SMS viewers can actually see a FRESHER og.png than iMessage viewers.

# Report 2: iOS Safari: Touch, Storage, SSE, Viewport

# iOS Safari Research Report: Hotspot Grid Painting App

Research date: September 2026. Target: iPhone Safari, iOS 17 through iOS 26.x. Each claim is tagged verified (primary source read), likely (credible secondary source or multiple corroborating sources), or unverified (plausible, could not confirm).

## 1. Touch drag painting: the battle-tested recipe

### Use pointer events, but know about implicit capture

**Verified:** The Pointer Events spec says touch input "SHOULD behave exactly as if setPointerCapture was called on the target element just before the invocation of any pointerdown listeners." This is implicit pointer capture: on touch, every subsequent `pointermove` targets the cell where the finger went DOWN, not the cell currently under the finger. (Read: [W3C Pointer Events spec, implicit pointer capture](https://w3c.github.io/pointerevents/#implicit-pointer-capture))

Consequence: you cannot rely on per-cell `pointerenter`/`pointerover` for paint-drag on iOS. The battle-tested pattern is:

1. Listen for `pointerdown` on the grid container. Record paint mode (adding vs erasing, based on the state of the first cell hit).
2. On `pointermove`, call `document.elementFromPoint(e.clientX, e.clientY)` (or compute the cell index from grid geometry, which is faster and more robust) to find the cell under the finger.
3. You do NOT need `setPointerCapture` for touch (it is implicit), but calling it is harmless and also covers mouse/pen. Handle `pointerup`, `pointercancel`, and `lostpointercapture` to end the stroke.
4. Computing cell from coordinates (getBoundingClientRect of the grid, divide by cell size) beats `elementFromPoint` because it keeps working when the finger drifts over gaps, borders, or overlays.

### Preventing scroll, rubber-band, and pull-to-refresh

**Verified:** `touch-action` on iOS Safari is real but historically flaky. A use-gesture issue documents that on iOS 15.x, even with `touch-action` set as documented, drags that were not perfectly axis-aligned got interrupted and the page scrolled; Chrome on iOS behaved correctly. The library's fix is a `preventScroll` option that uses non-passive touch listeners. (Read: [use-gesture issue 486](https://github.com/pmndrs/use-gesture/issues/486)) A related W3C issue documents that `touch-action: pan-y` regions on iOS get `pointercancel` during near-horizontal pans unless the gesture is very straight ([w3c/pointerevents #303](https://github.com/w3c/pointerevents/issues/303), search-level).

**Verified:** Apple's own forums confirm that pull-to-refresh in iOS 15+ Safari can be suppressed by calling `preventDefault()` in a NON-PASSIVE `touchmove` listener when the page is at scrollTop 0 and the finger moves down, and that `preventDefault()` cannot cancel a pan mid-gesture once Safari has claimed it. (Read: [Apple Developer Forums thread 697360](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/697360))

**Likely:** `overscroll-behavior` is supported since Safari 16 (Safari 16.4 fixed `overscroll-behavior: none` on pages too small to scroll, verified via [WebKit Safari 16.4 release notes](https://webkit.org/blog/13966/webkit-features-in-safari-16-4/)), but its implementation on the root element is still incomplete on iOS, so do not rely on it as the sole mechanism against rubber-banding ([CSS-Tricks](https://css-tricks.com/almanac/properties/o/overscroll-behavior/), search-level).

### The recipe (belt and suspenders, all layers)

CSS on the grid and every cell:

```css
.grid, .grid * {
  touch-action: none;              /* stop Safari claiming pans that start here */
  -webkit-user-select: none;       /* no text selection / loupe */
  user-select: none;
  -webkit-touch-callout: none;     /* no long-press callout sheet */
  -webkit-tap-highlight-color: transparent;  /* no gray tap flash */
}
```

JS:

```js
grid.addEventListener('pointerdown', startStroke);
grid.addEventListener('pointermove', moveStroke);
grid.addEventListener('pointerup', endStroke);
grid.addEventListener('pointercancel', endStroke);
// Safety net: while a stroke is active, swallow touchmove non-passively.
grid.addEventListener('touchmove', (e) => {
  if (strokeActive) e.preventDefault();
}, { passive: false });
```

Key gotchas, all likely-to-verified from the sources above plus longstanding WebKit behavior:

- `touch-action` must be present on the element BEFORE the touch starts. Setting it mid-gesture does nothing.
- `pointercancel` fires when Safari steals the gesture for scrolling; if you ever see it during painting, your `touch-action`/preventDefault layering has a hole. Treat it as end-of-stroke, never leave paint state dangling.
- The non-passive `touchmove` preventDefault also suppresses pull-to-refresh and rubber-band for gestures that begin on the grid, which is exactly the coexistence you want: page scrolls normally when touches start OUTSIDE the grid, paints when they start inside. Users scroll the page by grabbing the margins, headers, or a dedicated scroll gutter.
- `-webkit-user-select: none` prevents the long-press text loupe and selection handles; `-webkit-touch-callout: none` kills the long-press callout. There was an iOS 15 regression where a zoom callout appeared despite user-select none ([WebKit bug 231161](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=231161), search-level, since fixed), so keep both properties.
- If the grid must live in a page that also scrolls vertically, keep the grid narrower than the viewport or give a visible scroll rail, since a full-bleed `touch-action: none` grid taller than the screen makes the page unscrollable from within the grid. When2Meet-class apps accept this; a nicer pattern is `touch-action: pan-y` plus a short long-press-to-paint or first-cell-delay heuristic, but on iOS the pan-y directional lock `pointercancel` flakiness makes pure `touch-action: none` with constrained grid width the safer bet.

## 2. Haptics

**Verified:** iOS Safari does not support `navigator.vibrate` in any version through iOS 26.x, and desktop Safari never has. (Read: [caniuse.com/vibration](https://caniuse.com/vibration))

**Likely:** The only known workaround is the `<input type="checkbox" switch>` trick: toggling Apple's native switch control (shipped Safari 17.4) fires the Taptic Engine. Libraries create a hidden switch, toggle it inside a user-gesture event handler (click, pointermove, input), and remove it. It worked from roughly iOS 17.4/18.0 through iOS 26.4. **Apple patched it in iOS 26.5**, so on current iPhones it silently no-ops. (Read: [RapidToolSet iOS haptics tester](https://rapidtoolset.com/en/tool/ios-haptics-tester), [vibrator.dev](https://vibrator.dev/); the iOS 26.5 patch is reported by these third-party tools, not by Apple, hence likely not verified.)

Design guidance: do not build the feel of the app around haptics. Treat the switch trick as a progressive enhancement behind a wrapper that no-ops silently (libraries: ios-haptics, ios-vibrator-pro-max). There is no way to feature-detect whether the haptic actually fired, so never gate UX on it. What quality mobile web apps do instead: crisp sub-100ms visual microinteractions (scale pops, springy fills, color pulses per cell painted), optional tiny Web Audio ticks, and 60fps feedback that lands on the same frame as the touch. That is where the "game lobby" feel will come from.

## 3. Durable anonymous identity

**Verified:** ITP deletes ALL script-writable storage (localStorage, IndexedDB, script-set cookies via document.cookie, SessionStorage, service worker registrations and caches) after **7 days of Safari use without user interaction with the site**. The counter is days-of-Safari-use, not calendar days, so for a rarely used phone it stretches, but for a daily Safari user your localStorage device ID dies about a week after their last visit. (Read: [WebKit blog: Full Third-Party Cookie Blocking and More](https://webkit.org/blog/10218/full-third-party-cookie-blocking-and-more/), [WebKit Tracking Prevention Policy page](https://webkit.org/tracking-prevention/))

**Verified:** Script-set cookies (document.cookie) are capped at 7 days always, and at 24 hours if the user landed via a link decorated with known tracker click IDs. (Read: [WebKit tracking prevention page](https://webkit.org/tracking-prevention/))

**Verified:** Server-set first-party HTTP cookies are NOT subject to the 7-day cap. The WebKit tracking prevention page lists no lifetime cap for ordinary first-party Set-Cookie headers. The 7-day cap on server-set cookies applies only under CNAME cloaking (a first-party subdomain whose CNAME resolves to a third-party domain) and, per Safari 16.4-era updates, when the cookie-setting server's IP is third-party-ish relative to the site (search-level for the IP heuristic: [Stape ITP guide](https://stape.io/blog/safari-itp), likely). (Read: [WebKit tracking prevention page](https://webkit.org/tracking-prevention/))

**Likely:** Because the ID cookie will be set by meetup.sals.site itself (the exact host the user is browsing, in the document/API response), the CNAME cloaking rule does not apply even though the host resolves through Cloudflare's tunnel CNAME. That rule targets subresource hosts that differ from the browsed site. Do not set the cookie from a different subdomain (no api.sals.site cookie-setting).

**Best practice recipe for a months-durable device ID:**

1. Server sets `Set-Cookie: did=<uuid>; Max-Age=34560000; Path=/; Secure; SameSite=Lax` on the very first HTML response (400 days, the rfc6265bis cap Chrome enforces; Safari has no documented cap for compliant first-party cookies, likely).
2. Re-issue the cookie on every visit (rolling expiration), so the clock resets each time a friend opens any event link.
3. Mirror the ID in localStorage purely as a fast synchronous cache. On load: if the cookie exists but localStorage is empty (ITP purged it), rehydrate localStorage; if localStorage exists but the cookie is gone (rare), POST it back and have the server re-set the cookie. The server-set cookie is the source of truth; localStorage is disposable.
4. Store the display name server-side keyed by device ID, not only on the device, so a purged localStorage still greets the user by name once the cookie round-trips.
5. Accept graceful degradation: private browsing, "clear website data", and Safari-to-home-screen transitions all produce a fresh ID, and the cost is just retyping a first name. Home-screen web apps have their own storage and their own days-of-use counter, separate from Safari (verified for the counter, likely for full storage separation, per the WebKit blog above), so an installed copy will not see Safari's cookie.

## 4. SSE lifecycle on iOS plus Cloudflare

### iOS Safari behavior

**Likely (multiple corroborating sources, no primary Apple doc):** When the user backgrounds the tab, switches apps, or locks the screen, iOS Safari suspends network activity and drops the EventSource connection WITHOUT firing `onerror`, and `readyState` can remain OPEN (1) even though the TCP connection is dead. EventSource's built-in auto-reconnect therefore never triggers. Reports put suspension at roughly 30 seconds to 5 minutes after backgrounding. (Read: [server-sent-events.com cross-browser EventSource guide](https://www.server-sent-events.com/sse-protocol-fundamentals-architecture/browser-support-polyfill-strategies/cross-browser-compatibility-for-eventsource-api/); corroborated by [opencode issue 10721](https://github.com/anomalyco/opencode/issues/10721) and [dev.to SSE guide](https://dev.to/donbagger/server-sent-events-sse-explained-for-crypto-apps-with-real-examples-290), search-level)

**The reconnect pattern (battle-tested):**

- On `visibilitychange` to visible: unconditionally close and recreate the EventSource. Do not trust readyState.
- Watchdog: server sends a heartbeat comment (`: ping\n\n`) every 15 to 30 seconds; client records last-received time; if more than ~45s pass while visible, tear down and reconnect.
- Give events monotonic ids and honor `Last-Event-ID`, or simpler and more robust for this app: on every (re)connect, the server sends a full state snapshot as the first event, then deltas. With a heatmap this is small and eliminates replay bookkeeping.
- Presence design implication: the server only learns a connection is dead when a write to it fails. With 15 to 30s heartbeats, expect presence to lag reality by up to ~30 to 60s on abrupt disconnects (screen lock). Expire presence on missed-heartbeat-write failure, and treat `visibilitychange: hidden` as an optional instant "left" signal via `navigator.sendBeacon`.
- Connection budget: under HTTP/1.1, browsers cap ~6 connections per origin and each SSE stream eats one; Cloudflare serves HTTP/2+ to clients so this is mostly moot, but keep exactly one EventSource per tab (likely).

### Cloudflare specifics

**Verified:** Cloudflare's proxy read timeout (error 524) is now documented as 125 seconds for the origin to provide a response. (Read: [Cloudflare 524 docs](https://developers.cloudflare.com/support/troubleshooting/http-status-codes/cloudflare-5xx-errors/error-524/)) Community and practitioner guidance treats ~100s as the idle budget and recommends keepalives at half or less of that; 15 to 30 second heartbeats keep the stream alive indefinitely. (Read: [SmartScope SSE timeout mitigation guide](https://smartscope.blog/en/Infrastructure/sse-timeout-mitigation-cloudflare-alb/), likely)

**Verified:** cloudflared has a live 2025 bug where **Quick Tunnels buffer SSE-over-GET entirely** (events flush only when the stream closes) even with correct `text/event-stream`, `x-accel-buffering: no`, and chunked encoding; POST streams fine. Named tunnels (which meetup.sals.site will use) are the historically fixed path ([cloudflared issue 199](https://github.com/cloudflare/cloudflared/issues/199), search-level), but this is exactly the class of bug to smoke-test on day one through the real tunnel, not localhost. (Read: [cloudflared issue 1449](https://github.com/cloudflare/cloudflared/issues/1449))

Server checklist (likely, assembled from the above): `Content-Type: text/event-stream` with NO charset suffix (older WebKit rejected the charset parameter), `Cache-Control: no-store`, disable compression for the SSE route, flush after every event, heartbeat comment every 15 to 30s, and send a snapshot event immediately on connect so Cloudflare sees first bytes instantly (avoids any time-to-first-byte timeout). Hono's `streamSSE` helper fits this.

## 5. Viewport, keyboard, sticky bottom bar

**Verified-to-likely:** `100vh` on iOS Safari equals the LARGEST viewport (browser chrome hidden), so full-height layouts overflow when the URL bar is visible. `100dvh` tracks chrome show/hide dynamically. Critically, **no viewport unit reacts to the on-screen keyboard on iOS**: when the keyboard opens, iOS Safari does not resize the layout viewport; `window.innerHeight` and dvh stay the same while `visualViewport.height` shrinks, and iOS scrolls the visual viewport instead. ([dev.to on dvh and keyboards](https://dev.to/rl0425/why-css-dvh-ignores-the-mobile-keyboard-and-how-to-fix-it-31ao), [franciscomoretti.com](https://www.franciscomoretti.com/blog/fix-mobile-keyboard-overlap-with-visualviewport), search-level, consistent across many sources)

**Verified:** The `interactive-widget=resizes-content` viewport meta that fixes this on Android Chrome is NOT implemented in Safari (open WebKit bug). (Read via search results: [HTMHell advent article](https://www.htmhell.dev/adventcalendar/2024/4/), [WebKit bug 259770](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=259770))

**Recipe for the sticky bottom bar:**

- Viewport meta: `width=device-width, initial-scale=1, viewport-fit=cover`.
- App shell: `min-height: 100dvh` (with a `100vh` fallback line before it for ancient browsers).
- Bottom bar: `position: fixed; bottom: 0; padding-bottom: env(safe-area-inset-bottom)`.
- Keyboard: since this app has exactly one text input (the name), the cheap correct move is to hide or ignore the bottom bar while the name input is focused, or listen to `visualViewport` resize and translate the bar above the keyboard. Do not fight iOS here.
- Prevent iOS auto-zoom on input focus by giving the name input `font-size: 16px` or larger, instead of `maximum-scale=1` which harms accessibility (likely, longstanding behavior).

**Likely (multiple 2025-2026 practitioner reports, no primary Apple doc):** Safari 26 "Liquid Glass" changed chrome theming: `theme-color` meta is ignored for toolbar tinting; Safari now samples the `background-color`/`backdrop-filter` of `position: fixed`/`sticky` elements near viewport edges, falling back to the root background (transparent roots fall back to white bars). Fixed/sticky elements still tint even at `opacity: 0`, so hidden overlays must use `display: none`. Set an explicit `html, body` background color, and for a styled bottom bar keep the fixed wrapper transparent with visuals on an absolutely positioned child if you want to control the tint. (Read: [1ar.io Safari 26 Liquid Glass writeup](https://1ar.io/updates/safari-26-liquid-glass-web/); corroborated by [Jahir Fiquitiva](https://jahir.dev/blog/safari-toolbar), [Ben Frain](https://benfrain.com/ios26-safari-theme-color-tab-tinting-with-fixed-position-elements/), search-level)

## 6. Sharing, meta tags, PWA runway

**Verified:** `navigator.share({ title, url })` opens the native share sheet on iOS Safari; all three fields (title, text, url) are optional. Quirk: if you pass `text`, the share sheet's Copy action copies the TEXT, not the URL, so pass only `title` and `url` for a link-sharing app. Requires HTTPS and a user gesture (transient activation); feature-detect with `if (navigator.share)` and fall back to a copy-link button using the Clipboard API. (Read: [Adactio: The Web Share API in Safari on iOS](https://adactio.com/journal/15972); gesture requirement is spec-level, likely)

**Likely:** There are reports of iMessage dropping query strings from shared URLs ([Apple forums thread 724641](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/724641), search-level). Hotspot's short links should be pure path slugs (meetup.sals.site/e/abc123), no query params, which sidesteps this entirely and also makes the link prettier in the bubble.

**Meta tags worth setting:**

- OG for the iMessage embed: `og:title`, `og:description`, `og:image` (absolute URL, 1200x630), `og:url`, plus `twitter:card: summary_large_image`. iMessage link previews read Open Graph. Note iMessage caches previews per URL aggressively (unverified for exact TTL), so a "live" OG image will show state as-of-first-share for a given conversation; design the OG image to be great even when slightly stale.
- `theme-color` (still respected by pre-26 Safari and Android Chrome; harmless on Safari 26 which ignores it), with `media="(prefers-color-scheme: dark)"` variant.
- `apple-touch-icon` 180x180 (Safari uses it for home screen and favorites, verified via search-level sources).
- A minimal web app manifest plus `apple-mobile-web-app-capable`/`mobile-web-app-capable` costs nothing now and sets up the later PWA path.

**Verified:** Web Push on iOS requires iOS 16.4+ AND the site added to the Home Screen (standalone web app); permission must follow a user gesture. (Read: [WebKit: Web Push for Web Apps on iOS and iPadOS](https://webkit.org/blog/13878/web-push-for-web-apps-on-ios-and-ipados/), [Safari 16.4 release notes](https://webkit.org/blog/13966/webkit-features-in-safari-16-4/)) **Likely:** with iOS 26, sites added to the Home Screen open as web apps by default, and Safari 18.4 added Declarative Web Push (search-level: [brainhub.eu PWA on iOS](https://brainhub.eu/library/pwa-on-ios), [MagicBell guide](https://www.magicbell.com/blog/pwa-ios-limitations-safari-support-complete-guide)). Keep in mind for a future "notify me when the hotspot is decided" feature, but remember the identity split: the home-screen app will not share the Safari device cookie.

# Report 3: Competitor Teardown

# Competitive Teardown: Group-Availability Tools (2025-2026)

Research method: created a live test event on When2Meet and read its actual JavaScript; cloned and read source for Crab Fit, Timeful (formerly Schej), and Rallly; probed OG/meta tags with curl against real event pages on When2Meet, LettuceMeet, StrawPoll, Rallly, Crab Fit, Doodle, and Timeful; cross-checked closed-source flows (LettuceMeet, Doodle, StrawPoll) against multiple 2025-2026 guides and reviews.

Headline: **nobody in this market has presence, and only StrawPoll has any real-time transport at all** (a websocket for vote counts, not a grid). Nobody has a per-event OG image that reflects event state; the best anyone does is Rallly's static title-plus-author card. The entire 2025-2026 wave of micro-competitors (Carly, WhenItWorks, Toss-up, SyncWhen, Schedly) is attacking exactly two weaknesses: When2Meet's mobile painting and the lack of automatic "best time" surfacing. Hotspot's SSE liveness, presence, and live OG embed are unclaimed territory.

## 1. When2Meet (the incumbent, verified via live test event + JS source)

**Identity / taps.** No account. Per-event "sign in" with name plus optional password (`ProcessLogin.php` returns a numeric PersonID). Taps from link-open to painting: focus name field, type name, tap Sign In, paint. The "Your Availability" grid is hidden entirely until sign-in. Availability saves automatically on every drag-stop (`SaveTimes.php` posts a binary availability string on mouseup/touchend); there is no submit button, which people forget When2Meet got right.

**Grid interaction (mobile).** Verified from source: every cell is 44px wide and **9px tall**, with inline `ontouchstart/ontouchmove/ontouchend` handlers. Touch tracking uses `document.elementFromPoint` on each move, and `SelectToHereByTouch` calls `event.preventDefault()`, so any vertical finger drag on the grid paints instead of scrolling; the grid is a scroll trap. Selection is a rectangle between the start cell and current cell (col/row box), and mode (add vs erase) is decided by whether the first cell touched was already selected. 15-minute rows, days as columns, no erase tool (same gesture erases).

**Heatmap.** Group grid colors are a white-to-green (#339900) linear RGB ramp, normalized between the **minimum and maximum** observed counts, not 0..N. So a slot where 3 of 10 can make it renders pure white if 3 is the minimum anywhere; the scale is relative and misleading. Legend is a generated gradient bar labeled "Min/N Available" to "Max/N Available". Hover (or touch on mobile) over a group cell shows Available/Unavailable name lists in a left panel; that panel is off-screen on phones. With 2 participants it is effectively binary and fine; with 10 the mid-greens are indistinguishable. No best-time surfacing at all.

**Liveness.** None, confirmed in code: `AvailabilityGrids.php` (server-rendered HTML for both grids) is fetched only on initial load and when the participant changes the timezone dropdown. No polling, no sockets. You see other people's answers only on manual refresh.

**Share/link preview.** Verified: the event page has **zero OG tags** and no meta description. Texted link renders as a bare `when2meet.com` URL with the title "EventName - When2meet". Event URLs look like `when2meet.com/?38383620-8tsgW`.

**Timezone.** Better than its reputation: event has a home timezone; a participant timezone dropdown auto-selects the device zone (`Intl.DateTimeFormat().resolvedOptions().timeZone`) and re-renders the grid converted.

**What feels bad.** 2010 table-layout design, 9px touch targets, scroll-trap grid, two grids competing for a phone screen, relative color scale, hidden hover-only names, no best time, no link preview, PayPal donate button.

## 2. Crab Fit (open source, read from source at github.com/GRA0007/crab.fit)

Next.js frontend, Rust API (axum), pluggable storage adaptors (memory/SQL/Datastore). GPLv3.

**Identity / taps.** When2Meet's model modernized: per-event name plus optional password (bcrypt server-side), explicit Login button. After login it auto-switches you to the "You" tab to paint. Availability **autosaves on every pointerup** with optimistic update and rollback on error; no submit button.

**Grid interaction (mobile).** Pointer events with `touchAction: 'none'` set inline on every cell, and a clever `releasePointerCapture` on pointerdown so `pointerenter` fires on other cells during the drag. Same rectangle-select and first-cell-decides-mode as When2Meet. Same scroll trap: a finger on the grid always paints, never scrolls. Extras: Select All / None / Invert buttons plus Ctrl+A / Ctrl+Shift+A / Ctrl+I shortcuts; 15-minute rows with border styling to mark hour (solid) and half-hour (dotted) lines; grey placeholder cells for times outside the event window; column spacers between non-contiguous dates. Grid math runs in a web worker (they use the Temporal polyfill, which is slow).

**Heatmap.** The best in class mechanically. Palette is an alpha ramp of brand orange (transparent to opaque) via hue-map, with steps = (max-min)+1 and user-selectable colormaps (including colorblind-safe ones) in settings. Legend segments are hoverable: hovering a count isolates exactly those cells (everything else goes transparent). Person chips under the legend: hover to preview one person's availability, click to exclude/include them from the heatmap. Hover tooltip shows "3 / 6 available", the slot label, and names (unavailable names greyed). Clicking a cell copies "Available on [slot]: names" to the clipboard. A settings toggle outlines max-count cells ("highlight best times"). Still no ranked "best windows" list; you read the grid yourself.

**Liveness.** Marketing says "results update live". Code says otherwise: `getPeople` is refetched only when you switch to the Group tab. No SSE, no sockets, no interval. Confirmed none.

**Share/link preview.** Generic site-wide OG (title "Crab Fit", no image, `twitter:card summary`); per-event pages set only the page title. URLs are nice though: `crab.fit/pizza-night-123456` (punycode-encoded event name plus a random 6-digit number), generated server-side.

**Timezone.** The pattern to copy. Defaults to device timezone, converts the grid, and shows two subtle inline notes: "this event was created in [X], click to view in that timezone" and "your local timezone is [Y], click to switch back". One-tap toggles, no modal.

**Other steals in the code.** "Recents" store in localStorage lists events you have visited, and inside the editor you can **import your availability from a recent event** with one tap. Google Calendar and Outlook import buttons pre-fill busy times. Event mode supports both specific dates and recurring days-of-week. Two-way tab layout ("You" / "Group") means you cannot watch the heatmap evolve while painting, and on the You tab you cannot see anyone else.

## 3. LettuceMeet (closed source; probed event page + multiple guides)

**Identity / taps.** No account required; respond with a name (email optional, Google/Facebook sign-in optional for calendar overlay). The heavily-cited flow: land on the grid, drag your times, give your name to save. Roughly 2-3 taps plus typing. Editing an old response historically required the email/login path, a known annoyance.

**Grid.** When2Meet's paint-a-grid model with a cleaner, bigger, mobile-responsive UI: drag to paint green, "No earlier than / No later than" bounds at creation, specific-dates or days-of-week modes. Guides still recommend "keep the grid small" because long ranges get unwieldy on phones; drag vs scroll conflicts persist.

**Heatmap.** Green intensity ramp, darker = more people. Click a slot to see exactly who is free. A "Best Times" panel auto-surfaces the top overlap candidates, which reviewers consistently call out as the thing When2Meet lacks.

**Liveness.** None found; refresh-based. The page shell contains no socket or subscription code.

**Share/link preview.** Verified on a real event (`lettucemeet.com/l/beO1p`): the event page serves **completely generic OG tags** (title "LettuceMeet - Easy Group Scheduling", `og:url` pointing at the homepage) and the og:image is a broken relative path (`./favicon/og_image.png`) that scrapers will not resolve. A texted LettuceMeet link says nothing about your event. URL format is nice and short: `/l/beO1p`.

**Timezone.** Displays in the viewer's local timezone; thin documentation, no visible home-timezone note pattern.

**What feels bad.** No memory between polls (no persistent groups, every game night is a fresh poll re-shared manually), no realtime, anonymous-feeling generic links, coordination of the final pick happens off-platform.

## 4. Timeful, formerly Schej (open source, read from source at github.com/schej-it/timeful.app)

Vue 2 + Vuetify frontend, Go backend, MongoDB. The strongest modern When2Meet-style competitor; rebranded from schej.it (both domains live, confusingly serving identical meta).

**Identity / taps.** Optional Google/Outlook/Apple sign-in for calendar overlay, or "continue as guest" with a name. Guest name is remembered in localStorage **per event** (`${eventId}.guestName`), not per device globally. Flow: open link, tap "Add availability", choose guest, type name, paint, **tap Save**. An explicit save button and an explicit edit mode; roughly 4-5 taps plus typing.

**Grid interaction (mobile), the important pattern.** Painting is gated behind an explicit edit state (`allowDrag` is true only in EDIT_AVAILABILITY and similar states). In view mode, touch scrolls normally; once editing, `touchstart` on the grid calls `preventDefault()` and paints. Multi-finger touches are explicitly ignored (`e.touches.length > 1` returns, so two-finger scroll/zoom still works mid-edit, a nice touch). Same rectangle drag-box and first-cell-decides add/remove. 15-minute rows.

**Heatmap / features.** Green ramp with hover names; filter to a subset of people ("when can just these 3 meet"); a second availability state, **"if needed"**, rendered distinctly; creator-only visibility option; email reminders; CSV export; poll duplication; "availability groups" (persistent groups whose members' calendars stay synced, their answer to LettuceMeet's no-persistent-group complaint).

**Liveness.** None. Grep confirms no websocket, SSE, or polling interval on the event view.

**Share/link preview.** Static site-wide OG with a **relative** og:image path (`/img/ogImage.png`), which many scrapers drop. No per-event data in previews.

**What feels bad.** The Save button and mode toggle add taps and a "did it save?" doubt; Vue 2 app feel; the rebrand split its SEO and identity; per-event guest name means retyping your name for every new event.

## 5. Rallly (open source, read from source at github.com/lukevella/rallly)

Next.js, tRPC, Prisma. AGPLv3, self-hostable. A different paradigm: not an availability grid but a Doodle-style **option poll** (creator proposes specific dates/times, participants vote per option).

**Identity / taps.** No account to vote. Tap each option (vote selector cycles yes / if-need-be / no), then a New Participant modal asks name (required, max 100 chars), email (optional or required per poll setting), and an optional note, then submit. Taps scale with option count plus a 3-tap submit flow.

**Mobile.** Has a **dedicated mobile UI**: instead of shrinking the desktop table, options render as a vertical list grouped by date with a segmented control per row. No painting, so no drag/scroll problem at all, at the cost of expressiveness.

**Heatmap.** None; per-option vote tallies with progress bars. The creator gets a finalize step: pick the winning option and all participants are notified. Comments live on the poll page, which keeps the "so are we doing Friday?" conversation on-platform.

**Liveness.** No websocket/SSE (grep confirmed); tRPC + react-query means at most refetch-on-focus. Email notifications when people respond.

**Share/link preview.** The best in the category, verified in source: `generateMetadata` on invite pages requests `/api/og-image-poll?title=...&author=...`, a @vercel/og (satori-based) generated 1200x630 card with the Rallly logo, an "Invite" pill, "By {author}", and the poll title. Static branding, no live state, but a texted Rallly link actually looks like an invitation. This is the same satori approach Hotspot plans, so it is an existence proof, and also proof nobody has taken it further into live state.

**Timezone.** Automatic per-device conversion (DeviceDateTimeProvider server-detects, client adjusts).

**What feels bad.** Option-poll model front-loads work onto the creator (you must guess candidate times); increasingly SaaS-ified (spaces, billing, seats) which makes the free social use case feel like a trial; no visual density of a heatmap.

## 6. Doodle (closed source; 2025-2026 guides and probes)

**Identity / taps.** Creator now **must have an account**. Participants: no account, but name plus email to vote, then an explicit submit. Cookie banner, then **banner ads shown to your invitees** on free tier. "If-need-be" (maybe) voting is paywalled into Pro ($6.95/mo), and free group polls cap around 10 time slots.

**Interaction.** Table of options with yes (/no/maybe) ticks; calendar-connected creators see conflicts. Mobile apps exist and are competent but form-heavy.

**Heatmap.** None; tick table with the most-voted slot starred/highlighted.

**Liveness.** None; email notifications.

**Share/link preview.** Homepage og:image is literally a **48x48 SVG favicon** served from Contentful, verified via curl. For a company this size, texted links are remarkably bland.

**Timezone.** Account-level timezone and working hours; per-poll automatic conversion for participants.

**What feels bad.** It is the cautionary tale: every redesign added friction (account wall, ads at your guests, paywalled "maybe", slot caps). Multiple competitor blogs now rank "Doodle alternatives" as a top acquisition keyword, which tells you how its social users feel.

## 7. StrawPoll meeting polls (closed source; probed live poll page)

**Identity / taps.** No account; name plus tick options (yes/no/if-need-be), explicit Vote button. Editable votes and participant-visibility settings.

**Interaction / heatmap.** Doodle-style option table, not a grid. Best option = highest count.

**Liveness.** **The only tool with real realtime**: verified `wss://ws.strawpoll.com` websocket on a live meeting-poll page; results update live as votes come in, plus creator notifications. Proves live results are cheap and delightful; they just apply it to bar counts, not a heatmap, and there is no presence.

**Share/link preview.** Per-poll dynamic og:title (the poll question) but no og:image on poll pages. Short URLs (`strawpoll.com/k7swbr4d`).

**What feels bad.** Meeting polls are a side feature of a polling brand; utilitarian UI; nothing social or alive beyond the counter.

## 8. The 2025-2026 micro-competitor wave (WhenItWorks, Carly, Toss-up, SyncWhen, Schedly, Whocan, Plennur)

A cluster of small tools, all marketing through "When2Meet/LettuceMeet/Doodle alternative" SEO posts. Their converged pitch, almost word for word across sites: "designed tap-first, cells large enough to hit accurately", "best time auto-flagged, no manual heatmap interpretation", "real dates like Sat Apr 12, not generic weekdays", "one shared link, names only, no accounts", plus an organizer finalize/confirm step. Carly adds calendar-auto-filled grids and an AI email agent. None found with presence or live multi-user views. Market signal: mobile painting and hotspot-surfacing are the acknowledged wedge; liveness is not even on their radar.

## 9. Adjacent inspiration: Partiful

Not an availability tool, but the bar for "texted link that feels like an invitation": event links shared over SMS, RSVP with just a name and verified phone number, no app or account, playful design, guests react with emojis/GIFs, majority of users under 30, Google/Apple app awards and Time 100 Most Influential Companies 2025. Partiful proves Sal's core bet: for social groups the link IS the product, and the phone is the identity. (Their per-event link previews carry the event art; treat that specific claim as likely rather than verified, since no raw event page was probed.)

# STEAL LIST (ranked, most valuable first)

1. **Autosave-on-stroke with optimistic update and rollback (Crab Fit).** `onChange` fires on every pointerup, updates UI instantly, PATCHes the server, reverts on failure. Even When2Meet saves per drag-stop. Steal because it makes "no submit buttons anywhere" safe: worst case one stroke reverts, and combined with SSE everyone else sees each stroke land, which is your liveness demo.
2. **Timeful's multi-finger escape hatch: ignore `touches.length > 1` while painting.** One finger paints, two fingers scroll/zoom. Steal because it resolves drag-vs-scroll without Timeful's clunky edit-mode toggle: keep the grid always paintable (zero taps to start) but never trap two-finger scrolling; add scroll-safe gutters (time labels, day headers) as one-finger scroll zones. Every pure-paint competitor (When2Meet, Crab Fit) is a scroll trap; Timeful escapes it but charges 2 extra taps and a Save button. There is a free middle.
3. **Rallly's satori OG card, then lap them with live state.** Rallly's `/api/og-image-poll` (title, author, brand pill, 1200x630) is the category's best texted link and an exact existence proof for the planned satori + resvg stack. Nobody puts event state in it. A mini heatmap plus "4 of 6 responded" makes Hotspot links the best-looking scheduling links ever texted. One nuance: iMessage snapshots the preview at send time, so the embed shows state as of the share; design the card so that is a feature ("3 spots painted so far, tap to add yours") and re-fetch-friendly for later sharers.
4. **Crab Fit's rectangle drag-box with first-cell-decides add/erase.** Start cell already painted = the whole drag erases; otherwise it adds; selection is the rectangle between start and current cell, snapshotted against a start position (their `releasePointerCapture` + pointerenter trick, or elementFromPoint like When2Meet). Steal because it is the muscle memory every When2Meet user already has, and it gives erase with zero extra UI.
5. **Crab Fit's timezone microcopy.** Auto-detect device zone, convert everything, and show one quiet line: "Event created in America/New_York, view in that timezone" with a one-tap toggle back to local. Steal verbatim; it is exactly the "subtle note" in the brief and no modal or setting is needed.
6. **Interactive legend and person chips (Crab Fit).** Tap a legend segment to isolate slots with exactly that count; tap a person chip to include/exclude them ("when can just us four meet"); hover/long-press a cell for "3 of 6" plus names with unavailable names greyed. Steal the filtering, but make counts absolute 0..N (see Avoid #4) and surface names on tap since mobile has no hover.
7. **Ranked hotspot chips (LettuceMeet "Best Times", the whole 2025 wave).** Auto-surface top windows as tappable chips: "Fri 7-9pm, 5 of 6". Merge contiguous max-count slots into windows rather than listing raw 30-minute cells; every review of When2Meet cites the absence of this as its biggest flaw, and it is Hotspot's namesake.
8. **Device-remembered name, but global (upgrade of Timeful).** Timeful stores guest name in localStorage per event; Hotspot's per-device UUID plus cookie means you type your name once per phone, ever, and it is your edit credential. Steal the localStorage mechanism, kill the per-event scoping and the When2Meet/Crab Fit name+password ritual.
9. **Readable short slugs (Crab Fit).** `meetup.sals.site/pizza-night-4821` (slugified name plus short random suffix) reads as human in an iMessage bubble and is regenerable server-side. Crab Fit's punycode trick even handles emoji/unicode names.
10. **StrawPoll's live-results dopamine.** The only competitor with a socket uses it just for counters and it is instantly the most alive-feeling tool of the lot. Steal the spirit: animate heatmap cells as strokes arrive over SSE, tick the response count up in real time, and layer presence avum ("Sam is painting...") on the same connection, which nobody has.
11. **"Copy availability from your last event" (Crab Fit's Recents import).** LocalStorage recents list plus one-tap re-import of your own painted times. Cheap to build on the device UUID, and magic for friend groups that schedule monthly.
12. **Rallly's finalize-and-notify step (roadmap, not v1).** Every grid tool leaves "so, Friday then?" to the group chat. A creator-taps-the-hotspot "lock it in" state displayed on the live view (and OG image) closes the loop on-platform.
13. **Days-of-week mode naming (When2Meet, Crab Fit, LettuceMeet).** All three offer specific-dates vs recurring-weekday events. Hotspot's date mode covers Doodle-style whole days; keep recurring weekdays in mind as a cheap later addition since the grid machinery is identical.

# AVOID LIST (ranked, worst mistakes first)

1. **The scroll trap: unconditional `preventDefault`/`touchAction: none` on grid touches (When2Meet, Crab Fit).** Once the grid fills the viewport, the page becomes unscrollable on phones; users flick to scroll and instead paint garbage over three days. This is the single most-cited When2Meet mobile complaint. Any paint gesture must coexist with a scroll gesture (see Steal #2).
2. **Tiny touch targets: 9px-tall When2Meet rows and default 15-minute granularity.** 15-minute slots quadruple row count, shrink cells below finger size, and quadruple the data for no social benefit ("pizza night" is not scheduled to the quarter hour). Default to 30 or 60-minute slots with cells at 40px+; fine granularity is an option, not a default.
3. **Splitting "you" and "group" into separate tabs or modes (Crab Fit tabs, Timeful edit state, When2Meet side-by-side grids).** You either paint blind or watch passively; on a phone, side-by-side grids just make both tiny. Hotspot's single live view with your strokes overlaid on the group heatmap is the fix; do not regress into a mode switch.
4. **Relative min..max heatmap normalization (When2Meet).** A slot everyone can make and a slot only 3 of 10 can make span the full color range depending on the distribution; white does not mean zero. Use an absolute 0..N scale, give "everyone can make it" a distinct treatment, and always caption with numbers ("5 of 6"), never color alone. Also avoid Crab Fit's low-alpha ramp for low counts, which is nearly invisible on white.
5. **Explicit Save buttons and login gates before painting (Timeful's Save, When2Meet/Crab Fit's name+password sign-in, Doodle/Rallly submit modals).** Every gate before the grid loses respondents; Timeful's save step adds "did it stick?" anxiety. Also skip the optional per-event password entirely: it protects nothing anyone cares about at pizza-night stakes, and the device UUID covers edit rights.
6. **Generic or broken link previews (everyone except Rallly).** When2Meet has zero OG tags; LettuceMeet's event pages carry homepage metadata and a broken relative og:image; Timeful ships a relative og:image path; Doodle's og:image is a 48px favicon. In an iMessage-distribution product this is malpractice; also remember og:image URLs must be absolute.
7. **Monetization friction aimed at guests (Doodle).** Ads shown to invitees, account wall for creators, paywalled "maybe" votes, slot caps: Doodle converted the most beloved free social tool of the 2010s into the most-searched "alternatives to" keyword in the category. Whatever Hotspot monetizes later, never tax the invited friend.
8. **Claiming "live" without being live (Crab Fit).** Homepage says "results update live"; the code refetches only on tab switch. Users notice stale views in group chats ("I just filled it in, refresh"). Since Hotspot's whole identity is liveness, degraded SSE states need honest handling (reconnect indicator, last-updated stamp).
9. **Front-loading creator work with option-poll models (Doodle, Rallly, StrawPoll) as the primary flow.** Forcing the creator to enumerate candidate slots turns a 10-second share into a form session and caps what the heatmap can reveal. Candidate windows are fine as a creator hint (and Hotspot's hotspot output), not as the participant input model.
10. **No memory between events (LettuceMeet, and Timeful's per-event guest names).** Friend groups schedule repeatedly; making everyone re-type names and re-paint from scratch every time is the top LettuceMeet complaint. Device identity plus recents (Steal #8, #11) solves it; persistent named groups are the roadmap follow-on.
11. **Hover-dependent information (When2Meet's name panel, Crab Fit's tooltips, legend hovers).** Phones have no hover; any names/counts detail must be reachable by tap or long-press, and When2Meet's left-side panel placement is invisible on mobile anyway.
12. **Identity/brand confusion (Schej to Timeful rebrand, twin live domains with identical meta).** Pick the name once; the working codename is fine but do not ship two domains.
